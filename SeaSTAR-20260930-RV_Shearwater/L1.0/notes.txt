SeaSTAR-20260930-RV_Shearwater L1.0 -- aerosol optical depth, processing notes
Written 2026-10-27


Set up L1.0 for 20260930 and wrote L06-to-L10_sun.py. `make` in L1.0/scripts writes
pickle/SeaSTAR_<run>.L10 for all four runs. The unflagged data cover 20:37-22:38 UTC

1. WHAT'S IN L1.0

- Copied from L0.6: local.sh, channel_wavelengths.sh and the shared modules. environment.sh is
  adapted, with a new L10_DATA_DIR.
- seastar_error_flags.py gains the AOD_* flag bits.
- New parameters.sh: pressure fallback, tracking level, O3 300 DU, NO2 0.1 DU, the
  cross-sections and the layer heights.
- calibration.sh is written by the new calibration_vs_ref/scripts/write_v0_params.py. It holds
  the intercept V0s, the intercept a, and the scatter-quad temperature coefficients (mean of the
  two sites).
- calibration_vs_ref/scripts/transfer_v0.py now also writes the intercept a.
- New seastar_atmosphere.py: solar position, airmasses, Rayleigh, and gas optical depths.


2. HOW THE AOD IS CALCULATED

- The voltage is darksub_quad, temperature-corrected the same way the V0 was transferred.
- The calibration step is ln((V0 - a) / (d^2 * (|V| - a))).
- Rayleigh uses Bodhaine (1999) with the IMU pressure.
- Each constituent has its own airmass: Kasten & Young (1989) for Rayleigh, Kasten (1966) for
  aerosol, Komhyr (1989) for O3 and NO2.
- Solar position and earth-sun distance are computed from time and GPS position (GPS dropouts
  interpolated in time). The firmware elevation agrees to 0.004 degrees.
- The Angstrom exponent is fitted over 440-870 nm.
- Every timestep gets an AOD; aod_flags marks the ones to distrust.
- The .L10 files carry the L0.6 fields plus the intermediate terms, so any step can be rechecked.


3. RESULTS (09-32-14 run, 332 unflagged timesteps, medians)

    nm     340    380    440    500    675    870    1020   Angstrom
    AOD    0.138  0.150  0.126  0.122  0.104  0.099  0.101  0.37

- The other three runs have no unflagged timesteps. Every timestep is cloud-flagged, below
  CHANNEL_TEMP_MIN and has |V| <= a in at least one channel, which fits the field notes. Only
  332 of the 22,026 timesteps in the 09-32-14 run pass everything; cloud flags alone hit 21,689.
- The gas and Rayleigh terms check out against AERONET. Running the same algorithm on AERONET's
  raw voltages and V0s reproduces AERONET's AOD within +-0.006 at 340-870 nm at both sites. At
  1020 nm it is +0.002 at Ames and +0.017 at SLO; those offsets come from AERONET's 1020 data and
  don't involve SeaSTAR. The ozone and NO2 cross-sections are approximate values; this test is
  what shows they're adequate.


4. CAVEATS

- Most channels are very sensitive to the calibration. For ch2, 3, 5, 7 and 10, V0 - a is only
  about 0.3-0.6 V, so small errors in a or V0 turn into significant AOD errors, at fixed airmass
  mostly as a constant offset. The flat spectrum and the odd 340 < 380 ordering may come from
  this rather than from the air. Comparing with the 10:45 local Microtops reading would be the
  obvious test, but 10:45 falls in flagged data.
- The calibration is copied, not linked. write_v0_params.py has no make rule, same as
  write_tempcorr_params.py, so rerun it whenever v0_seastar.txt changes:
      cd calibration_vs_ref/scripts
      ./write_v0_params.py ../../SeaSTAR-20260930-RV_Shearwater/L1.0/setup/calibration.sh \
          ../fits/v0_seastar.txt ../fits/tempcorr_NASA_Ames.txt ../fits/tempcorr_San_Luis_Obispo.txt


5. AERONET CLOSURE CHECK (calibration_vs_ref/scripts/aeronet_aod_closure.py, `make closure`)

Runs the L1.0 AOD calculation on each site's AERONET voltages and V0s and compares the result with
AERONET's own AOD.

What it tests:
- It imports the campaign's own L1.0/scripts/seastar_atmosphere.py.
- It takes the ozone and NO2 columns, cross-sections, layer heights and channel wavelengths from
  that campaign's L1.0/setup, so it checks what L1.0 actually runs, not a copy.
- The campaign defaults to L10_CAMPAIGN=SeaSTAR-20260930-RV_Shearwater and can be changed on the
  make line.
- It screens dropouts from the V1/V2/V3 triplets the same way match_aeronet_seastar.py does, and
  reads V0 from the calibration file the npz records.
- Only the gas and Rayleigh terms and the airmasses are tested. SeaSTAR's calibration and
  temperature correction are not.

Outputs:
- calibration_vs_ref/fits/closure_<site>.txt: for each wavelength, the median difference
  (mine - AERONET), its interquartile range, the Rayleigh, O3 and NO2 optical depths used, and
  the slope of the difference against airmass.
- calibration_vs_ref/plots/closure_<site>.png: the differences against airmass.

Results (median mine - AERONET):

    nm          340      380      440      500      675      870      1020
    NASA_Ames  -0.0050  +0.0051  +0.0017  -0.0004  -0.0015  -0.0002  +0.0024
    SLO        -0.0010  +0.0045  +0.0023  -0.0057  -0.0029  +0.0000  +0.0168

The site pressures are guesses. They're in calibration_vs_ref/setup/parameters.sh as
AERONET_PRESSURE_NASA_Ames=1012 and AERONET_PRESSURE_San_Luis_Obispo=1005. AERONET uses the day's
NCEP pressure, which isn't in the export. A 1% error in pressure shifts the 340 nm AOD by about
0.007. Replace them if the sites' heights or the actual pressures are known.

What the plot shows: the differences fall on a separate curve for each day, shaped like a constant
divided by the airmass. That is the signature of a fixed offset in ln(V) for the day, a different
V0 or temperature correction, not an error in the gas terms. AERONET interpolates its V0 in time
between calibrations, and the export doesn't show the value it used. This probably explains most of
the residuals, including the +0.017 at 1020 nm at SLO, where the Si detector is the most
temperature-sensitive. The slope column in the table is a straight-line fit, so it doesn't capture
that 1/airmass shape well; the plot is the better diagnostic.

7. 500 NM AOD PLOT, AND SCREENING ON TRACKING ONLY (L1.0/scripts/plot_aod.py, `make plots`; added 2026-10-27)

plot_aod.py plots one wavelength's AOD (--nm, default 500) from all of a day's L10 files against
local time, into L1.0/plots/aod_<nm>nm_<date>.png. The timesteps that pass the screening are drawn
in colour, and the screened ones faintly behind them. Options:
- --screen: which aod_flags screen the data (cloud, tracking, temp_low, pressure, position,
  signal_low; default all). Screening by only some flags adds their names to the file name.
- --ymax: the top of the y axis.
- --hide-undefined: leave out the markers along the top of the plot for timesteps where |V| <= a
  (shown by default). The AOD is undefined there: ln((V0 - a) / (|V| - a)) is infinite at
  |V| = a and undefined below it, i.e. the sun is dimmed below the calibration's intercept by
  thick cloud. At 500 nm that's 13,210 of the 27,413 timesteps, 376 of them passing the tracking
  screen.
plot_aod.py needed PLOTS_DIR, which was added to seastar_filepaths.py.

The tracking screen (AOD_TRACKING) is set by AOD_TRACKING_LEVEL=1 in L1.0/setup/parameters.sh. A
timestep is screened if L0.5 set any of these in tracking_flags (L0.5/setup/parameters.sh):
- TRACKING_ERROR_1: the largest sun-to-target distance on the camera over the 1 s timestep plus
  0.25 s before it (ANALYSIS_MARGIN) is above TRACKING_EUCLIDIAN_MAX1 = 0.010
- NO_CAMERA_DATA: camera brightness 0 at some point in the timestep
- CAMERA_BRIGHTNESS_LOW / _HIGH: mean camera brightness outside 35-245
In the 09-32-14 run (22,026 timesteps), 18,566 are screened, all by TRACKING_ERROR_1 (one also has no
camera data). The looser levels would screen 7,237 (level 2, > 0.025) and 5,153 (level 3, > 0.05).

Findings:
- Most of the data screened by the full flags isn't cloudy. Between 14:30 and 15:00 local, 1,775 of
  the 1,800 cloud flags are AUTO_CLOUD_FLAG_FAIL, set when tracking was too poor for the cloud screen
  to run; it doesn't mean cloud was detected.
- Screened by tracking only, 3,134 timesteps pass, against 332 with all flags. The clear-sky 500 nm
  AOD forms a continuous baseline: about 0.09-0.12 just after 10:45, rising to about 0.15 by 11:25,
  then 0.12-0.15 from 13:30, apart from a dip to about 0.085 around 14:50. That agrees with the
  0.12-0.14 from the fully screened data. Cloud spikes up to AOD 2 around 11:20, 12:15-13:10 and
  13:20 match the cloud entries in the field notes.
- On a ship, level-1 tracking is probably too strict. TRIPLETVAR_TRACKING_LEVEL (L0.6) and
  AOD_TRACKING_LEVEL (L1.0) at 2 or 3 could recover much of the afternoon, but whether a tracking
  error of 0.010-0.025 changes the signal should be checked first, e.g. against the FOV campaigns
  (SeaSTAR-202609*-FOV-*). Neither setting has been changed.


8. V0_MODE: INTERCEPT OR ORIGIN CALIBRATION (added 2026-10-27)

V0_MODE in L1.0/setup/parameters.sh picks the calibration:
- intercept (the default, and what sections 3 and 6 used): |V| = a + b*V_aeronet,
  tau*m = ln((V0 - a) / (d^2 * (|V| - a)))
- origin: |V| = b*V_aeronet, tau*m = ln(V0 / (d^2 * |V|)), with V0_SEASTAR_ORIGIN_CH<n> from
  v0_seastar.txt and a = 0
Using the intercept V0 without removing a would be inconsistent; each V0 goes with its own model.

Changes:
- write_v0_params.py now writes both calibrations into calibration.sh: V0_INTERCEPT_CH<n>,
  V0_INTERCEPT_A_CH<n> and V0_ORIGIN_CH<n>. These replace the old V0_CH<n> and V0_A_CH<n> names;
  all six campaigns' calibration.sh files were rewritten.
- `make V0_MODE=origin` (or intercept) in a campaign's L1.0/scripts processes with that mode instead
  of parameters.sh's, into SeaSTAR_<run>_v0origin.L10 beside the usual SeaSTAR_<run>.L10. The mode is
  in each L10's metadata (V0_MODE).
- `make aod V0_MODE=origin` in calibration_vs_ref/scripts compares the tagged files with AERONET,
  into fits/aod_<site>_v0origin.txt and plots/aod_<site>_v0origin_<date>_*.png.
- plot_aod.py puts the mode in its title, and _v0origin in the file name in origin mode.
- The 20260922-20260929 L1.0 copies were brought back in line with this one (apart from ROOT_DIR and
  RAWFILES), so they also have plot_aod.py and `make plots`.
- Intercept-mode results are unchanged.

Why it matters: the intercept model says a 1% change in sunlight changes |V| by only (|V| - a)/|V|
percent, about 25-35% of it for ch2, 3, 5, 7 and 10 (a ~ 1.2 V, |V| ~ 1.6-1.8 V). The origin model
reads that small change in |V| as an equally small change in sunlight. For ch1 and ch6 (a ~ 0.02 V)
the two give nearly the same AOD.

Results (rms SeaSTAR - AERONET, worst of the five days 20260922-20260929):

    nm           340     380     440     500     675     870     1020
    intercept    0.015   0.009   0.011   0.007   0.005   0.004   0.021
    origin       0.026   0.011   0.084   0.061   0.025   0.015   0.034

- 440-1020 nm are 3-8x worse in origin mode. On Ames 22 Sep the origin-mode AOD falls steadily
  through the afternoon as the sun gets lower, going negative at 440 and 500 nm by 17:00, while
  AERONET stays flat at about 0.10. That fits a voltage that responds less than proportionally to
  the sunlight, as the intercept fit says, so the proportional model misreads the airmass-driven fall
  in signal as a change in aerosol. The origin-mode 440 nm trace is also very smooth, which fits a
  channel whose voltage barely responds.
- 340 and 380 nm are about the same in both modes. Origin mode is closer at 340 nm at SLO (median
  -0.002 against -0.011 to -0.016), suggesting ch6's small intercept (a ~ 0.015 V) isn't helping.
- Conclusion: the intercept calibration is clearly better for ch2, 3, 5, 7 and 10. For ch6 (340 nm)
  the origin calibration may be slightly better.

9. GAS ABSORPTION CROSS-SECTIONS (added 2026-10-27)

The ozone and NO2 cross-sections are fixed per-channel values in setup/parameters.sh (OZONE_XSEC_CH<n>,
NO2_XSEC_CH<n>, cm^2/molecule), used at each channel's centre wavelength. The gas optical depth is
tau = DU * 2.687e16 * sigma (seastar_atmosphere.tau_gas), with OZONE_COLUMN_DU=300 and NO2_COLUMN_DU=0.1.
It is multiplied by the ozone or NO2 airmass (22 km shell) and subtracted before dividing by the
aerosol airmass. The cross-sections and the taus are saved in each L10 (metadata OZONE_XSEC, NO2_XSEC;
fields tau_ozone_<nm>nm, tau_no2_<nm>nm).

    ch   nm     O3 sigma   NO2 sigma   tau O3 (300 DU)   tau NO2 (0.1 DU)
    6    340    9.0e-22    4.5e-19     0.0073            0.0012
    1    380    0          5.0e-19     0                 0.0013
    2    440    1.0e-22    5.5e-19     0.0008            0.0015
    3    500    1.3e-21    2.5e-19     0.0105            0.0007
    5    675    1.65e-21   1.5e-20     0.0133            0.00004
    7    870    4.0e-23    0           0.0003            0
    10   1020   0          0           0                 0

Caveats:
- The values are approximations of the standard laboratory sets (ozone: Bass-Paur/Serdyuchenko
  Huggins and Chappuis bands; NO2: Vandaele et al. 1998), probably good to 10-20%. They haven't
  been checked against the tables.
- They are taken at the centre wavelength, not weighted over the filter passband. This matters most
  for NO2, whose spectrum is very structured around 400-450 nm, given the 10 nm filters.
- There is no temperature dependence (this matters for the Huggins-band ozone at 340 nm).
- Only ozone at 500 and 675 nm matters much (0.010-0.013 of AOD): a 10% error in sigma or a 30 DU
  error in the column gives about 0.001 in AOD. NO2 at 0.1 DU is <= 0.0015 everywhere, but could be
  several times that in a polluted coastal plume.
- To do: compute filter-weighted cross-sections from the Serdyuchenko and Vandaele spectra and the
  SeaSTAR filter transmission curves, and use the day's satellite ozone/NO2 columns (e.g. OMI).

10. TRACKING SCREEN AFTER 13:30 LOCAL ON 20260930 (added 2026-10-28)

After 13:30 PDT (13:30-15:39, all in the 09-32-14 run; 7,760 timesteps), from the intercept-mode L10s:

                                                    timesteps   fraction
    AOD_TRACKING, level 1 (euclidian > 0.010)           5,200     67.0%
    would be flagged at level 2 (> 0.025)                 580      7.5%
    would be flagged at level 3 (> 0.05)                   66      0.9%
    NO_CAMERA_DATA / CAMERA_BRIGHTNESS_LOW / _HIGH          0      0%

- It is all pointing error: the camera was always present and in its brightness range. So the
  screen depends entirely on the threshold, and most screened points have a small error
  (0.010-0.025).
- It is spread through the afternoon: each half-hour loses 56-79% (13:30 68%, 14:00 56%, 14:30 79%,
  15:00 74%), and the partial half-hour from 15:30 loses 37%. There's no single bad stretch.
- Tracking isn't what removes most of the afternoon: the cloud flag is set on 95.7% of these
  timesteps. Of the 2,560 that pass tracking, 2,228 are removed by other flags (mostly cloud), and
  only 332 (4.3%) pass every flag.
- Relaxing AOD_TRACKING_LEVEL to 2 would cut the tracking loss to 7.5%, but the cloud flag would
  still remove nearly everything. To do: break the cloud flags down for this window, to see how much
  is AUTO_CLOUD_FLAG_FAIL (the cloud screen not running because of bad tracking) rather than cloud.

11. MICROTOPS OVERLAY, AND THE PLOT SETTINGS IN THE MAKEFILES (added 2026-10-28)

- --microtops <file> overlays a Microtops computed_aod_L15_groups file (orange diamonds). The file is a
  few header lines, then a tab-separated table with dates like SEP/30/2026 in UTC. The AOD at the plotted
  wavelength is used, and the times are converted to SEASTAR_TIMEZONE.
- --xmin HH:MM sets the start of the x axis, in local time on the plot's date.
The legend markers are now drawn at one fixed size (markerscale=4 had made the Microtops diamond huge).
