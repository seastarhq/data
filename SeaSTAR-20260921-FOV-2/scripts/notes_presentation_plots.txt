notes on the presentation FOV cut plots
2026-11-05, SeaSTAR-20260921-FOV-2

three scripts, two derived from cutFov.py and one from fitFov.py, all left
alone by the default `make` so the quicklooks are unchanged.  `make
presentation` builds all three, `make prescuts`, `make qcuts` and `make presfit`
build one each.


cutFov_presentation.py
----------------------
the cross-sections as cutFov.py draws them, with the channels relabelled and
the figure recast for showing to people.  writes

	<stem>_fov_cuts_presentation.png           the overlay, x cut and y cut
	<stem>_fov_cuts_channels_presentation.png  one channel per row

and a .pdf of the same name beside each.

channels.  ch8 and ch9 are the two 940 nm channels, in the water vapour band,
and ch4 is 550 nm; all three are dropped (excludedChannels at the top of the
script).  seven are left.  the mapping comes from

	hardware/configuration-sept-2026.md
	../../SeaSTAR-20260923-Ames-general_suntracking/L0.6/setup/channel_wavelengths.sh

	ch1 380   ch2 440   ch3 500   ch4 550   ch5  675
	ch6 340   ch7 870   ch8 940   ch9 940   ch10 1020

and is repeated in channelWavelengths in the script.  the plots are labelled
by wavelength, not by channel, and sorted by wavelength rather than by channel
number, so they run 340, 380, 440, 500, 675, 870, 1020.  the startup line still
prints both ("ch6 (340 nm), ...") so a label can be tied back to a log column.
a channel with no entry in the table falls to the end of the order and keeps
its ch<N> label.

range.  the auto-trimmed span was clipping the foot of the profile, badly on
the y cut, so --margin (default 4) keeps that many scan steps of background
either side of where the signal is.  clamped to the scan, so asking for more
than was scanned just plots the whole range.

layout.  the per-channel figure is one column of channels, two panels a row
(x cut, y cut).  the old two-halves-side-by-side layout and its spacer column
are gone.

titles.  none of the presentation figures has a title or a suptitle, in either
script; the slide carries that.  each panel of a per-channel figure has its
wavelength written inside the top left corner instead, since nothing else says
which channel a row is.

--counts-per-unit would add a second x axis across the top in encoder counts.
it is off and stays off: nothing in this repo gives a counts scale for the
stage.  sx/sy/tx/ty are a normalised 0-1 commanded position, the notes give
the scan ranges in those same units, and q1/q2 are the tracker's pointing in
degrees, not stage counts.  the plumbing is there if the scale turns up.


cutFovQ_presentation.py
-----------------------
the same cut drawn against the measured motor angle in degrees instead of the
commanded stage position.  writes

	<stem>_fov_cuts_q_presentation.png           the overlay, one panel
	<stem>_fov_cuts_channels_q_presentation.png  one channel per row

and a .pdf of the same name beside each.

method.  cells are still assigned on the commanded (tx, ty) grid, which is
exact; only the position each cell is drawn at changes, to the mean of the
angle over that cell's settled samples.  each drawn cut carries its own
angles, since a neighbouring row or column was scanned at a different time and
so at a different pointing.

q1 carries the scan, q2 does not.  from the 14:33 log:

	y cut (one column, fixed tx, about 2 minutes):
		q1 sweeps 43.4 -> 50.2 deg, monotonic, ~71 deg per stage unit,
		while the sun moves 0.3 deg.  this is a real FOV profile.

	x cut (one row, fixed ty, spread over the whole 46 minutes):
		q2 sits at 92.04-92.25 deg, 0.211 deg of non-monotonic wobble,
		no scan in it, while q1 slews 6.8 deg just following the sun's
		azimuth.  plotted, it is spaghetti.

so the x scan is not executed by q2.  the script therefore draws one cut only,
the y cut against q1 by default.  --cut x and --angle q2 still reach the other
combinations for checking; the script prints the span and monotonicity of
whichever axis it drew and warns when it spans under a degree.

parked rows.  the log opens with 1966 rows and closes with 3629 rows parked at
the commanded centre while the tracker runs.  those land in the centre cell,
which is the centre point of the cut, and swamp its mean angle (q1 45.77 deg
against a neighbour trend of about 46.9).  the script drops the stationary run
at each end by default, keeping the raster's own visit to that cell;
--keep-park disables it.  this matters only for the angle, but it cleans the
centre cell's channel average too.

axis.  --angle-range LO HI sets the view, 44 50 by default here; it crops only,
the cells drawn and the trimming are unchanged.  labelled ticks every degree,
unlabelled minor ticks and a light gridline every half degree (--minor-tick,
0 turns them off).  skipped on an axis too narrow to hold two of them, which
is what the degenerate q2 cut needs.  --width sets both figures' width in
inches, 6 by default.


fitFov_presentation.py
----------------------
the fit plot fitFov.py draws, with ch4, ch8 and ch9 dropped and the rest
labelled and sorted by wavelength.  writes

	<stem>_fov_fit_presentation.png    one panel per channel, and the legend

and a .pdf of the same name beside it.

channels.  the same seven as the cut plots; excludedChannels is at the top of
the script.  the dropped channels are not fitted at all, so they
leave the averaged centre as well as the figure, and the centre in the legend
is not the one on the quicklook.

layout.  four across, so seven channels and the legend fill a 2 x 4 grid.  no
titles, as with the other presentation plots: the wavelength is written inside
the top left of each panel.  the peak and the fwhm that were in the quicklook's
panel titles go to the printed table instead.  the axes read x and y, as on the
cuts, rather than tx and ty.

axis scale.  x and y are not drawn to the same scale.  the spot is about 1.4
times wider in stage y than stage x, so a unit of x is drawn that much longer
than a unit of y, which makes the half maximum contours come out round and the
panels square.  the scale is the median fwhm ratio of the channels drawn, one
value for all of them; it is printed when the script runs, but is not on the
plot.  round here is by construction, so it says nothing about whether the FOV is round on
the sky: there is no angle scale for x to check that with (see the q2 note
above).  --window is the half-width in x; y gets that times the scale.


make
----
	make presentation      all three scripts
	make prescuts          cutFov_presentation.py only
	make qcuts             cutFovQ_presentation.py only
	make presfit           fitFov_presentation.py only

variables, over and above the START_LOG / SETTLE / NEIGHBOURS / CUT_FLOOR /
CHANNELS the quicklooks already use, so the two sets of plots stay consistent:

	CUT_MARGIN       4          background steps kept either side
	COUNTS_PER_UNIT  (empty)    counts axis, off; see above
	ANGLE            q1         motor angle the q cuts use
	Q_CUT            y          which cut to take
	ANGLE_RANGE      44 50      degrees plotted; empty = the drawn cells
	FIG_WIDTH        6          width of both presentation figures

`make clean` removes these plots too.  `make show` prints them and the angle
settings.


watch out for
-------------
- colours are assigned by position in the channel list, so changing which
  channels are excluded shifts them.  dropping 550 nm moved 675 to purple,
  870 to brown and 1020 to pink.  the two presentation figures always agree
  with each other, but not with an older copy of either.
- on the q axis the faint neighbour cuts spread much wider than the centre
  cut.  that is real: neighbouring columns were scanned minutes apart, so the
  sun's motion offsets their q1 and shifts them sideways.  on the commanded
  position axis they sat on top of each other.  --neighbours 0 drops them.
- the q plots are specific to this scan's geometry.  before reusing them on
  another log, check the printed span and monotonicity line, and that the
  parked-row count looks like the park and not the scan.
